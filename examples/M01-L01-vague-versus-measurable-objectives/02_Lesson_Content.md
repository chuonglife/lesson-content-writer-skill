## 1. Lead-in (0:00-1:00)

**Purpose**: lead-in option 3, a story, built as a problem situation of the refutation kind (the belief "a good lesson is enough, the objective is paperwork"). The story is framed as made up. It connects to the aims teachers and trainers already write at the top of their plans.

She's made up, and so are her numbers. Priya writes that line at the top of Monday's plan and teaches all week. On Friday, 9 of her 27 pupils fail the test. A third of the class. So what failed, the teaching or the line? Don't answer yet. Keep it for minute six.

## 2. New content

### 2.1 What makes an objective vague (1:00-3:00)

**Key teaching points** (slide 4): a vague objective hides the action inside the learner's head. Six invisible verbs: understand, know, learn, be aware of, be familiar with, appreciate. The test: "What would I see?"

**Introduce**: Part one. Why a line like Priya's is vague, and a four-word test that catches it.

**Present**: [Show slide 4] Start with the verb. "Understand" happens inside a head. You can't watch it and you can't count it, so two teachers marking the same pupil will disagree. The same goes for know, learn, be aware of, be familiar with and appreciate. Six verbs. All invisible. [pause 2 seconds]

The test I use is four words long: what would I see? Try it on Priya's class. If her pupils understand fractions, on Friday she might see them put five fractions in order. Or she might see them shade three quarters of a shape. Those are two different lessons, and the vague line never picked one. I'll be blunt about "understand". It's a lazy verb. It lets the writer skip the hard decision and still feel finished.

[Show slide 5] Now a line that passes. "List the 4 steps of the fire drill in order." Ask the four words. You'd hear four steps and you could count them. Done.

**Apply**: [Show slide 6: three lines] Three lines are on screen. Pause the video and mark each one vague or measurable. Thirty seconds. [pause]

[Show slide 7: the answers] Line a, "Know the school's safeguarding policy", is vague: "know" is invisible. Line b, "Label the 7 parts of a lesson plan on a blank template, all 7 correct", is measurable: you'd see seven labels and you could check each one. Line c, "Be familiar with spreadsheet formulas", is vague. It tells a marker nothing.

**Check**:
> **Ask (pause the video)**: Line c again, the spreadsheet one. What might you see a learner do if it were true? Write one thing. Fifteen seconds. *Answer*: Anything you can watch counts. One example: "Write a SUM formula that totals a column of 12 marks." If your answer starts with "know" or "understand", it's still hiding.

**Lock-in**: Three terms to keep. Invisible verb. Hidden action. What would I see? [pause 3 seconds] *Bridge*: A verb you can watch is only half the repair.

### 2.2 Repairing a vague line (3:00-5:30)

**Key teaching points** (slide 8): the repair takes two moves. Move 1: a verb you can watch. Move 2: conditions (with what) and a standard (how well: a number, a time, or a reference to compare against).

**Introduce**: Part two. Repairing a vague line in two moves.

**Present**: [Show slide 8] Move one: swap the verb for something you can watch. Move two: add what a marker needs, which is with what and how well. Watch it on Priya's line. "Understand fractions" becomes "Order 5 fractions with different denominators from smallest to largest, without a fraction wall, at least 4 of 5 correct." It's longer. It also tells her what to teach and what Friday's test looks like.

Look at what move two added. "Without a fraction wall" is the with what. "At least 4 of 5 correct" is the how well. A standard is a number, a time, or something to compare against, like a template or a rulebook. If a stranger can't check it, it isn't a standard yet.

[Show slide 9] Now a near miss, and I think this one catches careful teachers more than the lazy verb does. "Explain fractions clearly." The verb is fine, you can hear an explanation. But "clearly" is an opinion. Whose? Swap it for something a stranger could check: "Explain to a partner why one half equals two quarters, using a drawing, in under 1 minute." A drawing and a clock. No opinion left.

It works outside school too. Say a sales coach writes "Know how to open a discovery call." Same two moves: "Open a discovery call with 2 questions about the client's week, inside the first 60 seconds." Two questions. Sixty seconds.

**Apply**: [Show slide 10] Pause the video and repair this one, written for a workplace trainer: "Be aware of the fire evacuation procedure." Write your version on paper. Ninety seconds. [pause]

[Show slide 11: one version that passes] Here's one version that passes. "List the 4 steps of the fire evacuation procedure in order, from memory, with no step missing." Yours will differ, and that's fine. Check two things in it: a verb you can watch, and something you can count. If yours has a number in it, you're most of the way there.

**Check**:
> **Ask (pause the video)**: In the version on screen, which words are the standard, and which are the conditions? Fifteen seconds. *Answer*: The standard is "in order" and "with no step missing". The condition is "from memory".

**Lock-in**: Keep these three. A verb you can watch. Conditions. A standard you can count. [pause 3 seconds] *Bridge*: One minute left, and you go first.

## 4. Consolidation (5:30-6:30)

[Webcam] Before I sum up, it's your turn. Pause and write the two moves from part two in your own words. Thirty seconds. [pause]

[Show slide 12] Now mine. A vague objective hides behind a verb nobody can see. The test is four words: what would I see? The repair is two moves: a verb you can watch, then conditions and a standard you can count. [pause 3 seconds]

Back to Priya and her 9 pupils. What failed, the teaching or the line? [pause 2 seconds] My view: the line. "Understand fractions" never said what Friday would ask, so she had nothing to aim her teaching at. One sentence at the top of a plan, and it steered the whole week. Tomorrow, before you plan a lesson, read its objective and ask the four words.

**Who**: you, alone. **What**: rewrite 3 objectives from your own lesson plans or course outline. **Where**: on this sheet. **How long**: about 12 minutes. **With what**: your own plans and the 3-line check below. **Product**: 3 rewritten objectives with the check column filled. **How it is presented and assessed**: post the 3 rewrites on the course Q&A board within 2 days; you pass when all 3 rewrites pass all 3 lines.

**The 3-line check**

1. The rewrite starts with a verb you can watch. It does not use understand, know, learn, be aware of, be familiar with or appreciate.
2. The rewrite says with what, or in what situation, the learner does it.
3. The rewrite has a standard you can count, time or compare against a reference.

If a rewrite gets "Not yet" on any line, rewrite it again before you post.
