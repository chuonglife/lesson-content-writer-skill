## Objectives

**By the end of this lesson, learners will be able to:**

### Knowledge
- State the 4 live-chat pacing rules of the Pellucid playbook (first reply within 60 seconds, no silence over 2 minutes, one idea per message of 3 lines or fewer, 2 chats at a time at most) and find the pacing breaks in a timestamped chat log handed out in class, finding at least 3 of the 4 planted breaks. [K1]
- Describe the 4 steps of the Pellucid de-escalation sequence (Hear it, Own the next move, Narrow it down, Commit and confirm) in order, without notes, and match each step to a chat line written for a given angry opening message, 4 of 4 correct. [K2]

### Skills
- Rewrite the 6 "fuel lines" on sheet M03-L2-3 into chat lines that follow the de-escalation sequence and the tone card from Lesson 1, with at least 5 of 6 accepted by the trio check (no blame word, no policy-first opening, 3 lines or fewer). [S1]
- Handle 3 role-played live chats with an upset customer in the Ticketyard sandbox, from scenario cards played by a partner, resolving each issue within 10 minutes using the knowledge base, with the first reply under 60 seconds, no gap over 2 minutes, and the customer's heat rating ending at 2 or lower on the card's 1-5 scale. [S2]

### Autonomy and responsibility
- Remain calm under pressure in all 3 role-played chats, including when the customer insults the agent or writes in capitals: no blame words, no sarcasm, no capitals, no more than one apology per chat, as recorded on criteria 6 and 7 of check sheet M03-L2-5. [A1]
- Comply with the identity check of the Pellucid playbook before sharing or changing any account detail, on every role-played chat, with no exception. [A2]

## Competency element

This lesson builds the competency element "Handle a live chat with an upset customer to resolution, keeping a calm and respectful tone". It is the live-chat half of the M03 skill objective "Write replies and live-chat responses that meet the quality rubric" and the whole of the M03 autonomy objective "Show care and a calm tone with frustrated customers". The 3 chat transcripts produced here are the warm-up for the M03 rubric-scored test (2 written replies and 1 live-chat role-play).

## What learners produce in this lesson

- A marked-up pacing log (sheet M03-L2-2, part A): evidence for K1.
- Two first messages written for a hot opener (sheet M03-L2-2, part B) and the quiz in the close: evidence for K2.
- The fuel-line rewrites (sheet M03-L2-3): evidence for S1.
- 3 Ticketyard chat transcripts as agent, each with an internal note giving the issue, the fix and the customer's heat start and end, scored on check sheet M03-L2-5: evidence for S2, A1 and A2.

## Assumptions

- The user said the lesson is taught live in a training room with about 12 new hires. The program file lists "Live online classroom with breakout pairs" for M03. The request wins: the lesson is written for an in-person room. Pairs sit back to back so the role-play stays text-only, which keeps the breakout-pair idea of the program.
- Company, product and helpdesk names are fictional: Pellucid Labs, Rotaplane (shift scheduling for small businesses, plans Starter, Team and Business), Ticketyard (helpdesk with a live chat module and a supervisor view). All customers and colleagues named in the scenarios are fictional too.
- The program does not spell out the chat rules or the de-escalation steps. The 4 pacing rules, the 4-step sequence, the identity check and the "one boundary line, then hand over to the team lead" rule are written here as the Pellucid playbook. A real company would replace them with its own playbook text.
- The 5 parts of a reply from Lesson 1 are taken as: greeting by name, acknowledgement, answer or steps, next step, sign-off. The tone card from Lesson 1 is assumed to be on every desk.
- The program's standard "keeping the customer calm" is made checkable through a heat rating: each card gives a starting heat (1-5) and the customer player records the end heat in the chat's internal note. Pass is end heat 2 or lower. The standards 60 seconds, 2 minutes, 3 lines and 2 chats are numbers chosen for this lesson and are tighter than, not contrary to, the program.
- Every card's issue has a knowledge base article in the sandbox that fixes it within 10 minutes. The cards were written to match M02's features and plans.
- Time per hour follows the skill's rule (theory 45 minutes, practice 60 minutes). The 10-minute break between 3.3 and 3.4 is not counted in the 165 minutes.
- Script length is planned at 150 spoken words per minute.
