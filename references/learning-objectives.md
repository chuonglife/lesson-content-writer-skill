# Writing the learning objectives of a lesson

Sources: course slides "Training Program Development" (Diep Phuong Chi, HCMC University of Technology and Education, 2026), in particular the slides "How to write learning outcomes", "How to write lesson objectives" and "How to write assessment standards inside objectives"; and Module 03 - Preparing to Teach (Vo Dinh Duong, HCMC University of Technology and Education, 2022).

## 1. What a lesson objective is

The objectives of a lesson **state the assessable abilities** learners will have when the lesson is over. They do not describe what the instructor will do ("introduce concept X", "help learners understand Y"). They describe **what the learner can do** afterwards.

The lesson is the finest of three levels (program, module, lesson), so its objectives must be measurable: anyone who reads them knows how to check whether a learner has reached them.

## 2. The required formula

```
Objective = (1) Action verb + (2) Object of the verb
          + (3) Means / conditions / context of performance
          + (4) Requirement / performance standard
```

- (1) and (2) are **always required**. Together they are called the *performance*.
- (3) and (4) are **required at lesson level** (the slides say a lesson objective has three parts: performance, conditions and standard). At program or module level they are only recommended.
- Fixed lead sentence: **"By the end of this lesson, learners will be able to:"** (allowed variant: "After this lesson, learners will be able to:").
- Every objective line **starts with the verb**. No subject, no "will be able to" repeated on each line. "Explain the attention curve..." is correct; "Learners explain..." is not.

## 3. The three groups

Always split objectives into these groups, in this order and with these names (they follow the national qualifications framework used in vocational education and training):

1. **Knowledge**: what the learner can *say, write, explain or analyse*.
2. **Skills**: what the learner can *do*, and what product they produce.
3. **Autonomy and responsibility**: the attitude and way of working the learner *shows* (takes initiative, follows rules, cooperates, works carefully, directs their own learning).

A short lesson (1-2 hours) usually has 1-2 knowledge lines, 1-2 skills lines and 1 autonomy line. A long practical lesson may have 3-4 skills lines. Never write more than 7 lines in total for one lesson: if you need more, the lesson carries too much and should be split.

## 4. Choosing the verb

Pick the level of ability the lesson really teaches. A lesson that introduces concepts sits at Remember/Understand. A lesson that shows how to do something sits at Apply or above. A synthesis or review lesson sits at Analyse/Evaluate/Create.

| Level (Bloom, revised, cognitive) | Verbs you can use |
|---|---|
| Remember | list, name, state, identify, define, describe, recall |
| Understand | explain, distinguish, compare, illustrate, summarise, paraphrase, classify |
| Apply | apply, use, carry out, operate, calculate, plan, set up, build |
| Analyse | analyse, determine, categorise, diagnose, select, contrast |
| Evaluate | evaluate, judge, appraise, critique, conclude, rate against criteria |
| Create | design, compose, develop, propose, organise, produce |

**Skills (Dave, psychomotor)**: imitation, then manipulation (following instructions), then precision, then articulation (coordinating several actions), then naturalisation (automatic performance). Verbs: handle, assemble, operate, design, build, record, edit, draft, present, control, organise, adjust, test.

**Autonomy and responsibility (Krathwohl, affective)**: receiving, then responding, then valuing, then organising values, then internalising them as a characteristic way of acting. Verbs: show, take the initiative, comply with, cooperate, direct one's own learning, accept responsibility, work carefully, respect.

**Banned** (not measurable): *understand, know, learn, be aware of, be familiar with, appreciate, grasp, realize*. When one appears, ask "What would the learner do to prove it?" and replace it with the verb of that action (understand becomes explain or compare; know becomes list or state; grasp becomes carry out correctly or apply).

## 5. Writing the conditions (part 3)

This answers: **with what, and in what situation** does the learner do it? Examples: "using the slide deck provided", "on a computer with software X installed", "in front of a class of 20-30 learners", "during a live online session on Zoom", "given a job analysis sheet", "from a module outline supplied by the instructor".

In a theory lesson the condition can be the information supplied: "given a sample lesson plan", "using the criteria table covered in class".

## 6. Writing the performance standard (part 4)

There are six kinds of standard. Choose one or two per line.

| Kind of standard | Question it answers | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Accuracy / tolerance | How exact must it be? | within ±2 mmHg; no more than 1% confusion |
| Speed / time | What time limit is acceptable? | within 5 minutes; 3 items per hour |
| Quality of the product | What must the finished product look like? | cut line flat, smooth, no burrs; no slide with more than 6 lines |
| Reference to a document or rule | According to which rule, guideline or standard? | following the manufacturer's manual; using the institution's template |
| Rate / quantity to reach | How many, what percentage? | distinguishes 100% of the symbols correctly; completes 3 drawings |
| Procedure / sequence | Are the steps done in the right order? | carries out all 4 steps; mounts the blank and centres it before grinding |

A standard must be something the instructor can check **by looking, counting or comparing against a reference**. "Good", "effective", "engaging" and "appropriate" are not standards. Turn them into observable signs ("at least 2 interactive questions in 10 minutes", "a listener in the back row can read the text").

## 7. Reference examples from the source material

**Practical lesson, skill "Measuring blood pressure", nursing.** *By the end of this lesson, the future nurse will be able to:*
Measure the blood pressure of a real (or simulated) patient during a routine ward round, within 5 minutes; identify the patient correctly; produce a reading within ±2 mmHg of the instructor's reading; report immediately to the head nurse if the pressure is outside the normal range; record the result clearly on the patient's chart.

**Practical lesson, skill "Identifying faults in a shaft", machine-tool repair fitting:**
- Determine the technical requirements of the shaft to be inspected from the file or drawing.
- Draw up an inspection process sheet for each type of shaft, suited to the production conditions.
- Find all faults in the shaft using the tools and equipment of a typical repair workshop.
- Rate the severity of each type of fault against the standard.

**Theory lesson "Resistors" (lower level):**
- Pick out all resistors from a mixed set of components; tolerance no more than 1% error.
- Read the correct value of any colour-banded resistor in no more than 30 seconds.

**Theory lesson "Resistors" (higher level):**
- Determine the maximum and minimum resistance values that can be assigned to a position in an amplifier circuit diagram so that the output parameters of the circuit do not change.

## 8. Worked examples written with the formula

**Example A. Teacher-training lesson "Opening a lesson so it holds attention"** (2 hours, for teachers and trainers). *By the end of this lesson, learners will be able to:*

**Knowledge**
- Describe the attention curve in a class period (strongest in the first 5 minutes, drops in the last 15) and 4 ways to open a lesson from learners' own experience, well enough to explain why a given opening worked or failed.
- Distinguish a *problem-posing* opening from a *story* opening in 3 sample openings, getting 3 of 3 right.

**Skills**
- Draft a 3-5 minute opening for one of their own lessons that links to prior experience, poses a problem situation and states the objective; written as spoken script of no more than 300 words.
- Deliver that opening to a group of 5 in exactly 5 minutes, with at least one question aimed at the audience and eye contact with every listener.

**Autonomy and responsibility**
- Take the initiative to record the group's "three comments each" feedback and revise the script before submitting it.

**Example B. Corporate onboarding lesson "Handling a live chat from an upset customer"** (90 minutes, new support agents). *By the end of this lesson, learners will be able to:*

**Knowledge**
- List the 4 steps of the company's de-escalation sequence in order, without notes.

**Skills**
- Handle a simulated live chat with an upset customer, using the CRM sandbox and the approved macro library, resolving or correctly escalating it within 8 minutes and with a first reply in under 60 seconds.
- Log the contact in the CRM using all 5 mandatory fields, with no field left blank.

**Autonomy and responsibility**
- Follow the data-privacy script before sharing any account detail, on every simulated chat.

## 9. Self-check before delivering

- [ ] The lead sentence "By the end of this lesson, learners will be able to:" is present.
- [ ] All three groups appear (Knowledge / Skills / Autonomy and responsibility), in this order and with these names.
- [ ] Every line starts with an action verb; no banned verb appears.
- [ ] Every line has parts (1)(2)(3)(4), and part (4) can be checked.
- [ ] Every objective has at least one activity in the lesson that teaches it and one question or task that tests it.
- [ ] If the lesson belongs to a program designed with the companion skill curriculum-designer (its `program.json` has `modules[].lessons[].objectives.knowledge/skills/autonomy`), the objectives keep the intent of the ones recorded there. You may rewrite them to the full 4 parts, but never contradict them.
