# Assessment inside a lesson

Sources: Part 4 of the textbook *Teaching Methods* (HCMC University of Technology and Education, 2007); Module 03 - Preparing to Teach (Vo Dinh Duong, 2022), Lesson 2 section 2.4 and Lesson 3 section 2.5.

## 1. Principles

Assessment takes the **lesson objectives as the standard**: every objective line needs at least one question, task or criterion that tests it, and nothing outside the objectives gets tested. Assessment is objective, comprehensive (knowledge, skills, attitude), frequent (start of session, after each section in the Check step, end of lesson), and its results are used to fix the teaching. An instrument must meet three tests: **validity** (it measures what it claims to), **reliability** (any marker reaches the same result) and **usability**.

Assess competence against **criteria** (criterion-referenced): compare with the standard, never learners with each other. Criteria and indicators are **published in advance**. In training, the standard is the minimum needed to do the job.

## 2. Oral questioning (used in the Check step of each section and in consolidation)

Three levels of question (Costa), mixed in a proportion that rises with the objectives:
- **Recall**: complete, define, list, observe, retell, select. ("Name the 4 parts of a lesson objective.")
- **Processing**: analyse, compare, explain, organise, sequence. ("Which part of this procedure matters most? Why?")
- **Application**: apply, give an example, predict, generalise, evaluate. ("If your class were an online group of 40, how would step 3 change?")

Five steps: ask the whole class, wait a few seconds and watch, invite a volunteer or name someone, **listen** and respond (correct / partly correct / wrong / no answer: fix the answer, not the person), then seek agreement on the answer. Ask narrow, concrete questions first and broad, abstract ones later. Prepare the answer and the marking scale for every question.

## 3. Written assessment

- *Short-answer questions*: one point per question, 10-15 minutes, answers straight to the point in bullet form. Suits the end of a theory lesson.
- *Essay*: 1-3 hours, with opening, body and close; only for a module's final assessment.
- Announce in advance; prepare several versions of equal difficulty; mark with comments that explain typical errors; return work within 1-2 weeks.

## 4. Objective tests (end-of-lesson quiz, quizzes in an online course)

Five steps: objectives, then content analysis (main ideas, basic concepts), then an **objectives x content matrix**, then writing the items, then trying them out and revising.

Four item types and their rules:
- **True-False**: 50% chance of a lucky guess, so keep it to 20% of the total score or less; a false statement should contain only one false element; avoid copying the textbook; avoid giveaway words ("always" and "never" are usually false, "sometimes" and "may" usually true); no double negatives; roughly equal numbers of true and false.
- **Multiple choice**: usually 4 options, the same in every item of the test; one correct answer (if several options are partly right, write "best answer"); distractors equally attractive; random position for the correct answer; no more than 60% of the items.
- **Matching**: more options in the right column than the left (3 prompts against 6-10 options); each column has a heading and holds one kind of item; state the relationship clearly.
- **Fill in the blank**: blank in the middle or at the end of the sentence; basic knowledge only; blanks of equal length; not too many blanks in one sentence.

Use two test versions with a different order and a separate answer sheet. For an online course: a quiz of 5-10 items after every 1-2 videos, with an explanation for each answer.

## 5. Assessing practice (practical and integrated lessons)

Two kinds, usually both:
- **Process check** with a **checklist**: steps in the right order (as in the job sheet), correct technique with tools, compliance with rules, safety and workplace organisation.
- **Product check** with a **rating scale or rubric**: shape, size, quality, quantity, time, tolerance.

Six steps to prepare: define the situation, the skills to assess, the materials and tools, the performance standard, the strategy (whom to observe, when, how many at once, usually 2-6), then build the instrument.

Writing criteria: **result noun + quality indicator + measure**, in the passive voice. Source example: "The cloth is cut to the correct size of 1 m, the cut is smooth and straight, and the edge is not torn." Example for teacher training: "The opening is delivered in 3-5 minutes, includes at least one question aimed at the audience, and states the objective as something the learner will be able to do." Criteria must always cover **time**, **effectiveness** and **safety or rules** (in teaching: professional standards, copyright, protection of learners' data).

Template for a skill assessment sheet:

| No. | Competence element | Criterion and indicator | Best evidence | Met | Not yet met | N/A |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

## 6. Grading and scales (when marks are needed)

The Vietnamese 10-point scale: Excellent 9-10; Very good 8 to under 9; Good 7 to under 8; Fairly satisfactory 6 to under 7; Satisfactory 5 to under 6; Weak 4 to under 5; Poor under 4. Pass mark 5.0. Converting a multiple-choice score: d = D/K x k (D = maximum of the target scale, K = maximum of the test, k = score obtained). For online courses, pass/fail against a rubric plus a quiz pass mark of 70% is common; state the threshold in the content file.

When you know the grading convention of the user's institution or platform (pass/fail rubric, percentages, quiz pass mark), use that instead of the scale above.

## 7. Writing the assessment part of the lesson content file

The "Assessment" section of each lesson contains:
1. An **objective <-> instrument** table: for each objective line, which question, quiz item, task or criterion tests it, and in which activity.
2. The oral questions used in the lesson, with answers.
3. An end-of-lesson quiz (theory or online lessons): 5-10 items, answers, a short explanation each.
4. A skill assessment sheet or product rubric (practical or integrated lessons), published to learners beforehand.
5. Homework: the product, the deadline, the marking criteria.
