# Structure of a Lesson

Source: Module 03 - Preparing to Teach (Võ Đình Dương, HCMC University of Technology and Education, 2022). The three lesson plan forms come from Vietnam's vocational education system (forms 05, 06 and 07 under Decision 62/2008/QĐ-BLĐTBXH). From here on they are simply the theory, practical and integrated lesson plans. The skill uses this structure as the **backbone of the lesson content file**: the reader follows the same sequence the instructor will teach.

## 1. Choose the lesson type

| Lesson type | Signs | Most important part |
|---|---|---|
| **Theory** | Objectives are mostly knowledge (concepts, classification, structure, principles, rules); learners make nothing with their hands during the session | New content |
| **Practical** | The objective is a skill or procedure; learners must produce something or perform an operation; the underlying knowledge was taught in an earlier lesson | Initial instruction (1/4-1/3 of the time) + Ongoing coaching (more than 50%) |
| **Integrated** | Teach the knowledge needed, then practice right away in the same session to build one working competence. **Most lessons in a typical course are this type.** | Problem solving (3 tasks) |

Lesson names: *verb + object* ("Write measurable lesson objectives", "Handle an angry customer on live chat", "Take a manual blood pressure reading") or a gerund phrase ("Opening a class with an attention hook"). One meaning, few words, with the core skill inside.

Time rules: no more than **3 specific objectives per 45 minutes**; a 2-hour lesson (90-120 minutes) takes at most 5-7 objective lines; each sub-section runs 7-15 minutes.

## 2. The common frame for every lesson

```
Header: lesson name (CAPS), previous lesson, duration, objectives (Knowledge / Skills / Autonomy and responsibility), materials, class organization
I.   Settle the class - Review the previous lesson (2-5 min; practical/integrated lessons add ground rules and safety)
II.  Teaching sequence (table: No. | Content | Instructor activity | Learner activity | Time)
III. Post-lesson reflection (4-5 questions to ask yourself after teaching)
References
```

### Part II by lesson type

**Theory lesson**: 1 Lead-in -> 2 New content -> 3 Consolidation and closing -> 4 Self-study assignment.

**Practical lesson**: 1 Lead-in -> 2 Initial instruction -> 3 Ongoing coaching -> 4 Closing debrief -> 5 Self-practice assignment.

**Integrated lesson**: 1 Lead-in -> 2 Topic introduction -> 3 Problem solving -> 4 Problem close -> 5 Self-study assignment.

## 3. Lead-in (3-7 minutes)

Pick **one of 11 options** from Module 03:

1. Review the previous lesson, then pose the problem
2. A current real-world event
3. A story
4. A role-model example
5. A picture or photo set
6. A film clip or simulation
7. A sound or recording
8. A demonstration of a neat trick
9. A game or activity
10. A shocking statistic
11. A creative combination of the above

For an integrated lesson, the lead-in must **pose a problem situation** tied to the core of the lesson.

After that, three things always follow: write the **lesson name** on the board or screen (and keep it there all session); announce the **objectives**; present the **structure and plan** of the lesson. Check any lead-in with **G-L-O-S-S**:

- **G**et attention
- **L**ink with the experience learners already have
- **O**utcomes: what they will be able to do
- **S**tructure: the order of activities
- **S**timulation: why it is worth learning, which real job it solves

## 4. New content (theory lesson): small sections, each run as an IPAC cycle

Split the content into **small, self-contained sections**. For each one, extract the *keywords, main idea and core focus* (the "key teaching points", which are exactly what goes on the slide, board or handout). Teach each section with the **IPAC cycle**:

1. **Introduce**: name the section and show the key teaching points through both hearing and sight.
2. **Present**: deliver them with the lead method (guided questioning: ask -> learners think and answer -> instructor analyzes and locks in the answer as the new knowledge; or explanation plus visuals; or active reading...).
3. **Apply**: an example, or a small exercise applying what was just learned.
4. **Check**: test how well this section landed (1-2 questions, a quick quiz, a lightning round) before moving to the next.

Sequences by content type:

- **Concept, by induction (5 steps)**: example (given by the instructor or the learners) -> analyze and compare from several angles, adding a **non-example** -> prompt learners to generalize into a definition -> sharpen the definition -> practice applying it. **By deduction** (when the concept is already in the material): study the definition -> find the essential features -> examples and non-examples -> classify a series of cases -> practice.
- **Classification**: for each category give its signs, characteristics, function, strengths and weaknesses in context, and relations to other categories -> exercise: choose the right category for a situation.
- **Structure / anatomy** (a lesson plan, a sales page, a slide deck): observe the whole -> separate each part (name, purpose, requirements) -> relations between parts when "in operation" -> improvement trends -> learners redraw the diagram themselves.
- **Principle** (why method X works): scientific basis -> function of each part -> overall principle, stressing where the "essence" lies -> examples of correct and incorrect use -> how to adjust -> conditions of use. Teaching a principle by an **indirect route** (learners do or observe, then discover it) works better than telling it directly.
- **Rules / regulations / procedures (written)**: a violation or a situation that needs a decision -> look up the text -> draw out the rule -> apply it to 2-3 other situations.

## 5. Initial instruction (practical lesson): 3 tasks

Takes 1/4-1/3 of the lesson time. In this order:

1. **Review the related knowledge and analyze the procedure** step by step; stress the **checkpoints** (places where the learner must stop and confirm the result is acceptable before going on) and the **key points** (the trade secret that makes the skill click fast); introduce tools and materials. Use an information sheet or slides.
2. **Demonstrate** following the procedure (3 runs / 2 runs / 1 run depending on difficulty; video or simulation can support).
3. **Consolidate the procedure and technique**: ask check questions; invite at random **at least 20% of the class** to describe the procedure and try one step, reaching level 2 "can do"; analyze **common faults, how to prevent and fix them**; safety; assign positions and set time allowances.

**Ongoing coaching** (more than 50% of the time): learners practice on their own following the job sheet; the instructor moves person by person or group by group, steps in as soon as something goes wrong, pays attention to weaker learners, spreads time evenly; the level of intervention **decreases** over time; learners record results on the sheet.

**Closing debrief**: end practice before the bell; comment on the operations and results against the objectives; the mistakes most people made and how to fix them; real-world use; cleanup and safety; preparing for the next lesson.

## 6. Problem solving (integrated lesson): 3 tasks

- **Topic introduction** (after the lead-in): lesson name on the board all session; objectives with the focus stressed; an **overview of the procedure or work steps** to be done.
- **Task 1 - The job knowledge needed**: each knowledge objective is one theory problem, taught with the Introduce - Present - Apply - Check cycle; give **just enough knowledge** to do the work step; check understanding before moving on.
- **Task 2 - Guide the performance**: the procedure step by step with checkpoints and key points -> demonstration -> consolidation, common faults, how to fix them. Not every job needs a demonstration: a job sheet or "try - fail - redo" may do.
- **Task 3 - Practice**: individually, in pairs or in groups following the job sheet; the instructor supervises and helps on the spot; **assess with a skills check sheet** at the end of the stage before moving on to another skill.
- **Problem close**: consolidate knowledge; consolidate skill (faults, fixes); comment on results; prepare for the next lesson; have learners self-assess against the procedure sheet and the product sheet.

## 7. Consolidation and self-study assignment (a few minutes, do not overdo it)

Consolidation: summarize; highlight the main points *against the objectives*; condense into something memorable (3 keywords, a diagram, a mnemonic); invite questions; spot which objectives have not been met so they can be filled in or studied alone.

Self-study assignment: homework with a **product, deadline, method, materials and tools**; **differentiated** tasks by level; preparation for the next lesson; a note about working at home without the classroom's equipment.

## 8. Post-lesson reflection (write the questions in advance for the instructor to answer after teaching)

Theory: were the methods right for each section? Did the materials fit the method and the content? How was the time per section? What needs to change in the class organization and in how understanding is checked? Practical/integrated: add the forms of practice and cleanup and safety.

## 9. Mapping the lesson plan to a self-paced video course

For a self-paced video course (Udemy, Teachable, Coursera, LMS).

| Lesson plan | Self-paced video course |
|---|---|
| Settle the class, review, lead-in, topic introduction | Video 1 (6-9 minutes): 1-2 minute review through pause questions; lead-in; lesson name; the promise; roadmap of videos, quiz and assignment |
| Each small section (Introduce - Present - Apply - Check - Lock-in) | One video of 10-12 minutes at most; Apply = "pause the video and..."; Check = a pause question whose answer is spoken afterwards |
| Quiz after a group of theory sections | A platform quiz of 5-10 questions with explanations, pass mark stated clearly (usually 8/10) |
| Guide the performance: procedure + demonstration + faults | One demonstration video (screen recording), 2-3 runs; learners try one step on the spot at a pause point |
| Supervised practice | A submitted assignment following the job sheet; instructor feedback within 7 days; decreasing support = correcting only one line of the sample |
| Skills check with a sheet | A self-scored checklist submitted with the product + the instructor's rubric |
| Consolidation, closing, self-study | Final video (5-8 minutes): learners write their own summary in Q&A first, then lock in against the objectives, differentiated homework, preparation for the next lesson |
| Post-lesson reflection | Questions about course data: video completion rate, quiz items under 60% correct, common errors in submissions |

## 10. The lesson file package (what the content file and the materials must contain)

An outline of the "key teaching points" for each section; the four teaching sheets (information / task / job / check), with a header of title, sheet number, date and class; the **job sheet** as a table *No. | Step name | What to do | Equipment - tools - materials | Image | Criterion*; the skills assessment sheet; the slide outline; self-practice exercises.
